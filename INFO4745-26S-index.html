Course Description

This course introduces students to the basic concepts and components of Information Architecture (IA) within the context of end-user and organizational needs. It provides students with an understanding of the intellectual technologies necessary to design effective and cost-efficient information systems such as digital libraries, database systems, web-accessible resources, and collaborative computer systems in organizational environments.

Students conduct a collaborative term project to design and prototype a real-world information system, integrating the knowledge and skills learned in organization of information, visual design, human interface, and usability.

Course Objectives

  • Understand the major concepts and principles of IA: key terms and definitions, components of IA, project development, and strategy in professional settings.
  • Develop a holistic view of IA including design, research, prototyping, usability testing, and evaluation.
  • Apply user-centered design methodology and document the design thinking process for IA projects.
  • Demonstrate mastery of IA research methods (e.g., Card Sorting, User Interviews) and consult professional resources for IA research and strategy.
  • Create IA documentation (e.g., User Persona, Use Cases) and utilize IA implementation tools.
  • Design and prototype a web-based information system in Figma, and evaluate its usability following IA best practices.

Final Project — Team IA Case Study

Students work in teams of 3 to evaluate and redesign the information architecture of an assigned generative AI education product, producing a professional IA case study with a mid-fidelity Figma prototype and portfolio. Each team assigns three roles: UX Content Strategist, UX Design Specialist, and User Research Specialist. The project runs across all ten weeks, and the three deliverables build on one another so the final case study demonstrates the complete IA process from research to prototype.

  • Deliverable 1 — Project Plan & IA Research: project plan, lightweight user research (card sorting or user interviews), a current-state IA audit of the assigned product, and a persona and use case.
  • Deliverable 2 — IA Strategy & Design Documentation: the IA strategy; the redesigned organization, labeling, navigation, and search systems; and design documentation including blueprints, wireframes, content map, content inventory, and controlled vocabulary.
  • Deliverable 3 — Figma Prototype, Usability Evaluation & Final Case Study: a mid-fidelity Figma prototype, a lightweight usability evaluation, and the consolidated IA case study (merging Deliverables 1 and 2 with revisions) presented as a portfolio, with a short team video. Due at the end of Week 10 (Jul 24).

The evaluation concerns the product's information organization and navigation structure, not the quality of the AI's answers or its teaching effectiveness. At the start of Deliverable 1, each team must fully screenshot the assigned product as a baseline, since GenAI products change quickly. Each member must demonstrate individual contribution at each stage; grades reflect both individual and team work. Use of generative AI to produce a student's own writing or design work will result in 0 points.

Course Policies

Discussions

Original posts must be submitted by Friday midnight; replies to classmates by Sunday 11:59 PM CT. Each post should be 200–250 words with APA 7th edition citations. Students must post their original before seeing replies.

Late Work

Each student has a one-time excuse for late submission of individual work. Any additional late work receives a zero automatically. Group work and final project deliverables are not accepted after the due date under any circumstances.

Resubmission / Correction

Students have a one-time opportunity to make corrections to individual work (does not apply to Module Discussions, Project Chats, or Quizzes). Contact the instructor via Canvas within 48 hours of grade release. Resubmission does not guarantee a higher grade.

Communication & Feedback

Use Canvas Messages for all course communication. The instructor responds Tue & Thu 9:00–11:00 AM. Assignment grades and feedback are provided within one week of the due date.

Academic Integrity

Generative AI use on assignments, discussions, or the final project results in 0 points. Per UNT Policy 06.003 and IS guidelines: First offense — zero on the assignment; Second offense — F for the course. See IS departmental guidelines.
